Programmes

Six areas of work. Each has its own objectives and its own timeline, and each is designed to produce something a health system can use — a running pipeline, a validated tool, a trained cohort — rather than only a paper.

Programme 1

Vector-borne disease and climate–health surveillance

Dengue and other vector-borne diseases in India move with rainfall, temperature, humidity and land use. The data to see that coming exists — in meteorological archives, satellite products and national programme reporting — but rarely in one place, at district resolution, and never in a form a programme officer can act on in the week that matters.

  • Operate and expand a national spatiotemporal dengue and vector-borne disease analysis pipeline that integrates climate and environmental data.
  • Automate ingestion of meteorological and health-indicator data — IMD, WHO GHO, World Bank, NASA POWER and comparable sources — into a maintained, queryable database.
  • Publish periodic risk assessments and forecasting outputs in a form usable by national and state vector-borne disease control programmes.
Programme 2

Artificial intelligence in medical education

Indian medical colleges have far more students than faculty hours. AI tools are already in students' hands, mostly unevaluated and mostly not aligned to the competency-based curriculum they are being examined on. We would rather build the aligned version and measure whether it works.

  • Develop and refine a retrieval-augmented and agentic AI assistant for community medicine and allied subjects, evolving into a multi-tenant platform other institutions can adopt.
  • Digitise and modernise teaching charts and information, education and communication materials for community medicine instruction.
  • Evaluate learning outcomes from AI-assisted teaching and publish the findings, including the negative ones, to guide adoption elsewhere.
Programme 3

Antimicrobial resistance surveillance and stewardship

Most Indian hospitals generate resistance data every day and store it in a form nobody can analyse across time. Stewardship decisions then get made on impression. The first useful step is unglamorous: get one laboratory's data into a structured, standards-aligned database and keep it there.

  • Build institutional AMR databases starting from microbiology laboratory data, with a roadmap toward a broader model for stewardship policy simulation.
  • Align data collection with WHO GLASS and the national action plan on AMR wherever feasible.
  • Support antibiotic stewardship research and policy recommendations at partner hospitals.
Programme 4

Wearable and point-of-care health technology

A monitoring parameter that requires a laboratory requires a laboratory-equipped hospital. Moving even one such parameter to the bedside changes who can be monitored, and where.

  • Advance wearable health-monitoring device concepts developed through structured faculty development and research programmes.
  • Progress a continuous, non-invasive or minimally invasive blood gas monitoring concept through benchtop prototyping toward a defined regulatory pathway under CDSCO, ISO 13485 and IEC 60601.
  • Document and protect intellectual property arising from device development in a way consistent with the Foundation's not-for-profit, public-benefit purpose.
Programme 5

One Health and health systems research

Zoonotic spillover, vector range shifts and occupational exposure are not separate subjects with separate datasets. Studying them together is the only way to see the driver rather than the symptom.

  • Support integrated human–animal–environment research relevant to zoonotic and climate-sensitive disease transmission.
  • Conduct occupational and environmental health studies — including ergonomic and exposure risk in informal-sector work — that inform workplace and public health policy.
  • Contribute methodological and field capacity to multi-institutional ICMR- and NMC-funded studies across South India.
Programme 6

Capacity building and community engagement

Every pipeline above needs people who can run it after the grant closes. Training is not an add-on to the research programme; it is how the research programme survives.

  • Run short-term studentships and mentored research programmes for MBBS and allied health students.
  • Conduct faculty development in research methodology, AI literacy and grant writing.
  • Deliver community health screening, awareness and health education activities free or at subsidised cost in our areas of operation.
In development

Testing and certification for health technology

A stated object of the Foundation, and the piece of this work with the clearest gap in the Indian market.

Wearable and consumer digital health devices now make clinical-sounding claims — oxygen saturation, arrhythmia detection, sleep-stage classification, blood pressure — and reach Indian users well ahead of any independent evidence that those claims hold on Indian populations, in Indian conditions, at Indian price points. Regulatory clearance, where it applies at all, is not the same as performance validation.

We intend to build an independent function that does four things:

  • Develops test protocols and benchmarks for wearable, digital and point-of-care health technologies, published openly so manufacturers can design against them.
  • Runs technical, clinical and safety validation against those protocols, including performance across skin tones, body habitus, ambient conditions and comorbidities that international validation studies routinely under-sample.
  • Issues certificates of conformity under the applicable frameworks — the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation, the Bureau of Indian Standards, ISO 13485 and IEC 60601 among them.
  • Publishes what it finds, so that procurement officers, clinicians and buyers can compare devices on evidence rather than marketing.

On independence

The Foundation also develops its own devices. A body that certifies technology cannot be casually adjacent to a body that builds it. Accreditation standards for certification bodies are explicit about this, and the Memorandum of Association commits the Foundation to maintaining whatever structural separation those standards require — separate governance, separate personnel, and no certification of our own products by our own assessors.

Where this stands

This is an object of the Foundation, not an operating service. Before it becomes one, we need incorporation, accreditation or empanelment from a competent authority such as NABCB, BIS, CDSCO or QCI, and either an equipped testing laboratory or a formal partnership with one. We will say plainly on this page when each of those is in place.
