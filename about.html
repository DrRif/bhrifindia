Why a foundation, and why this one

Most of the work described here already happens — in medical colleges, in laboratories, in individual PhD projects. What it lacks is a body that can hold a grant, keep a database alive between studies, and carry a device from a bench prototype to a regulatory file. That is what the Foundation is for.

Vision

An India in which every individual — whatever their geography, income or access — benefits from timely, evidence-based, technology-enabled health research and health professions education; where disease surveillance, medical training and public health innovation are integrated, data-driven, and responsive to the realities of climate change and emerging biological threats.

Mission

Bridge Health Research Foundation exists to bridge the gap between health research, medical education and health systems practice. Concretely, that means five commitments:

  • Conducting rigorous, field-relevant research on infectious and vector-borne diseases, antimicrobial resistance, climate–health linkages and health systems performance.
  • Designing and deploying appropriate digital and wearable health technologies, including AI-based tools, for surveillance, diagnosis and monitoring.
  • Strengthening medical and allied health education through innovative curricula, simulation-based and AI-assisted teaching tools, and structured research training for students and faculty.
  • Building multi-institutional and multi-sectoral partnerships — academic, governmental and community — that translate research into policy and practice.
  • Making the outputs of this work openly available, affordable and usable in low-resource public health settings.

The name

A bridge is not decoration here. Health knowledge and health practice sit on separate ground, and the gap between them is real — measured in years, in districts, and in decisions taken without the evidence that already exists. A span has to be anchored on both sides to carry anything across. So we do not run research and then hope someone applies it; we build the surveillance system, the teaching tool and the device alongside the study, with the people who will use them.

Our identity carries that literally: a health anchor and a research anchor, with the things that connect them — data, artificial intelligence, One Health, health outcomes — riding the cable between.

Objects of the Foundation

These are the main objects as drafted for Clause III(A) of the Memorandum of Association. They set the outer limit of what the Foundation may lawfully do.

  1. To promote, undertake, conduct, sponsor and support scientific and applied research in public health, epidemiology, communicable and non-communicable diseases, vector-borne diseases, antimicrobial resistance, environmental and climate–health linkages, and allied biomedical, clinical and population health sciences, and to disseminate the findings for the benefit of the public, the scientific community and policymakers.
  2. To research, design, develop, test, validate and deploy digital health technologies, artificial intelligence and machine learning systems, wearable devices, point-of-care and minimally invasive diagnostic tools, and health data platforms for the early detection, surveillance, monitoring and management of disease, and to make such technologies accessible and affordable in low-resource and underserved settings.
  3. To act as a testing, evaluation, quality-assurance and certification agency for wearable, digital health and point-of-care medical technologies — developing testing protocols, standards and benchmarks, conducting technical, clinical and safety validation, and issuing certificates of conformity under applicable regulatory and standards frameworks — subject to maintaining such independence from the Foundation's own technology-development activities as accreditation standards require.
  4. To promote, support and undertake innovation and excellence in medical and allied health professions education, including curricula, e-learning and simulation-based training tools, AI-based educational and research-assistant platforms, and structured research-training and mentorship programmes.
  5. To establish, strengthen and operate disease surveillance systems, health and environmental data infrastructure, and research databases, in collaboration with academic institutions, government departments, and national and international health agencies.
  6. To promote and practise the One Health approach by integrating human health, animal health and environmental research, surveillance and intervention programmes.
  7. To build research and technical capacity among students, clinicians, scientists, and community and public health workers through training programmes, fellowships, short-term studentships, workshops, conferences and structured mentorship.
  8. To collaborate with, and seek recognition, accreditation, funding or affiliation from, government bodies, academic and research institutions, hospitals, and national and international agencies, for the advancement of public health research, medical education and health systems strengthening.
  9. To undertake health policy research, advocacy and knowledge-translation activities, and to prepare and disseminate reports, briefs, guidelines and recommendations that inform evidence-based public health policy at local, state and national levels.
  10. To promote indigenous innovation in healthcare technology, and to support the protection, responsible licensing and public-benefit dissemination of intellectual property arising from it.
  11. To organise, conduct or support community health screening, awareness, education and outreach, and to provide health information, education and communication materials to the public free or at subsidised cost, without profit motive.

The Memorandum also carries the usual incidental and ancillary powers — to receive grants, donations and CSR contributions; to publish; to hold property and equipment; to enter into collaboration agreements; to hold and license intellectual property; to seek accreditation as a testing or certification body; to employ staff; and to invest funds in the manner permitted to a Section 8 company.

Legal form and compliance

The Foundation's not-for-profit character is a constraint written into its constitution, not a description of current intent.

Legal form
Company licensed under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 — incorporation in progress.
Corporate identity number
To be published on allotment.
Registered office
Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu, India. Full address to be published on incorporation.
Area of operation
The Union of India, with power to establish offices, centres, field sites or collaborations anywhere in India and, where the objects require it, abroad.
Application of income
All income and property is applied solely towards the objects. No dividend, bonus or other profit is paid or transferred, directly or indirectly, to any present or past member. Reasonable remuneration for services actually rendered is permitted.
On winding up
Any property remaining after debts and liabilities are satisfied is not distributed among members. It is transferred to another company with similar objects, as determined by the members or, failing that, by the Tribunal.
Tax exemptions
Registration under sections 12A and 80G of the Income-tax Act, and CSR eligibility, will be applied for after incorporation. Status will be published here.

Documents filed with the Registrar of Companies, and the Foundation's annual reports and audited accounts, will be published on this page once available.

People

The Foundation is being established by faculty working in community medicine, public health research and health technology across South India. Board and founding member details will be published on incorporation.
