A finding published in Delhi does not automatically become a decision made in a taluk hospital.

Bridge Health Research Foundation exists to close that distance. We run field research, build the tools that carry its findings, and train the people who will use them — as one continuous piece of work rather than three separate ones.

Research

Field-relevant, not shelf-bound

Vector-borne disease, antimicrobial resistance, climate–health linkages, occupational and environmental exposure, and health systems performance — studied at the scale decisions are made at: the district.

Technology

Built for the constraint, not around it

Surveillance pipelines, AI and machine learning systems, wearable and point-of-care devices, and health data platforms designed from the start to run on the budgets and bandwidth of public health settings.

Education

The people who carry it forward

Curricula, simulation and AI-assisted teaching tools, and structured research mentorship for undergraduates, postgraduates, faculty and frontline health workers.

Everything we produce is meant to be used. Our default is open publication, affordable licensing, and formats that a district programme officer can act on without a data scientist in the room.

Six programmes on one cable

Each area runs independently, but they share data, methods and people. That is deliberate: the climate data behind a dengue forecast is the same data behind an AMR seasonality model, and the students trained on one become the field teams for the other.

Programme 1

Vector-borne disease and climate–health surveillance

A national spatiotemporal analysis pipeline for dengue and other vector-borne disease, integrating meteorological, environmental and health-indicator data into a maintained, queryable database, with forecasting outputs written for programme use.

Programme 2

Artificial intelligence in medical education

A retrieval-augmented and agentic AI assistant for community medicine and allied subjects, evolving into a multi-institution platform — alongside digitised teaching charts, IEC materials, and published evaluation of what AI-assisted teaching actually does to learning outcomes.

Programme 3

Antimicrobial resistance surveillance and stewardship

Institutional AMR databases built up from microbiology laboratory data, aligned with WHO GLASS and the national AMR action plan, with a roadmap toward district-level stewardship policy simulation.

Programme 4

Wearable and point-of-care health technology

Device concepts taken from bench prototype to a defined technology readiness level with a documented regulatory pathway — including continuous, minimally invasive monitoring for parameters that currently need a laboratory.

Programme 5

One Health and health systems research

Integrated human, animal and environmental research on zoonotic and climate-sensitive transmission, plus occupational and environmental health studies in informal-sector work that feed directly into workplace and public health policy.

Programme 6

Capacity building and community engagement

Short-term studentships and mentored research for MBBS and allied health students, faculty development in research methodology, AI literacy and grant writing, and free or subsidised community screening and health education.

In development

An independent testing and certification arm

Wearable and digital health devices reach Indian users faster than the evidence for them does. We intend to build an independent testing, evaluation and quality-assurance function for wearable, digital and point-of-care health technology — developing test protocols and benchmarks, running technical, clinical and safety validation, and issuing certificates of conformity under the relevant CDSCO, BIS, ISO 13485 and IEC 60601 frameworks.

This will be structurally separated from our own device development, to the degree accreditation standards require. It is a stated object of the Foundation, not yet an operating service.

How this will work

What we intend to have built by 2031

Our first five-year plan, written so it can be checked against.

  • Recognition as an ICMR- and NMC-engaged research entity able to host and administer multi-institutional grants across South India.
  • Two surveillance or health-data pipelines running with real-time or near-real-time reporting.
  • The AI medical education platform validated and in use across partner medical colleges.
  • At least one wearable or point-of-care device advanced from prototype to a defined technology readiness level with a documented regulatory pathway.
  • A structured AMR surveillance and stewardship programme linked to live laboratory data at one or more partner institutions.
  • A funding base that combines competitive grants, institutional collaboration and CSR or philanthropic support, rather than depending on any one of them.
  • A cohort of student and early-career researchers mentored every year through studentships, fellowships and short-term research programmes.
